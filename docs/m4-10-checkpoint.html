4.10 Module checkpoint

Choose the right test, run it, and interpret it critically

NoteHow this checkpoint works

Part A tests whether you can choose the right analysis. Part B is a mini-project on the squad data: three research questions, three analyses, and a critical look at a colleague’s claim.

⏱️ About 45 minutes.

Part A · Which analysis?

Do U19 players improve their 10 m sprint time after 8 weeks of resisted-sprint training? (Same players tested before and after.)

The same players are measured twice: a paired design. See lesson 4.3.

Does the injury rate (injured yes/no) differ between goalkeepers, defenders, midfielders and forwards?

Both variables are categorical, so you compare counts in a 4 × 2 table. See lesson 4.8.

Does VO₂max differ between three groups of runners (sprinters, middle-distance, marathon)?

3+ groups with a numeric outcome → ANOVA; three separate t-tests inflate the false-positive rate. See lesson 4.4.

Is the relationship between sprint time and jump height still present after accounting for body mass and sex?

“After accounting for” = adjusting for other variables, which is what multiple regression does. See lesson 4.7.

How consistent is a new jump mat across two trials performed 5 minutes apart?

Reliability needs absolute (TE, CV, limits of agreement) and relative (ICC) measures. A correlation alone ignores systematic bias. See lesson 4.9.

Part B · Mini-project

Question 1: Do the sports differ in eccentric (unweighting + braking) time?

  1. Run a Welch t-test of ecc_time_s by sport and store it in tt.
  2. Calculate Cohen’s d (d) as (mean basketball − mean soccer) / pooled SD.

t.test(ecc_time_s ~ sport, data = squad)

Question 2: Is the sport difference in jump height robust?

Fit a multiple regression of jump_height_m on sport, body_mass_kg and cmj_depth_m (model). Extract the 95 % CI of the sport coefficient (ci_sport) with confint(model)["sportSoccer", ].

lm(jump_height_m ~ sport + body_mass_kg + cmj_depth_m, data = squad)

Question 3: A colleague’s claim

A colleague writes in a draft paper:

“Net propulsive impulse was the strongest determinant of jump height (R² = 0.92, p < 0.001), indicating that coaches should prioritise impulse-oriented training.”

  1. Calculate net_imp = con_impulse_rel − 9.81 × con_time_s and verify the colleague’s R² (r2_net).
  2. Calculate the take-off velocity v_to from jump height and its correlation with net_imp (r_coupling).

squad$con_impulse_rel - 9.81 * squad$con_time_s, sqrt(2 * 9.81 * squad$jump_height_m), cor(squad$net_imp, squad$v_to).

squad$net_imp <- squad$con_impulse_rel - 9.81 * squad$con_time_s r2_net <- summary(lm(jump_height_m ~ net_imp, data = squad))$r.squared squad$v_to <- sqrt(2 * 9.81 * squad$jump_height_m) r_coupling <- cor(squad$net_imp, squad$v_to) round(c(r2_net = r2_net, r_coupling = r_coupling), 3)

What’s the best revision of the colleague’s sentence?

Changing the statistic doesn’t fix mathematical coupling. The analysis has to be redesigned around variables that are not definitions of the outcome. See lesson 4.5.
